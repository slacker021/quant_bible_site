If $m \in \mathbb{Z}$ but $m \ne 0,1$, then assuming first that $m \in \mathbb{N}$ yields the result showing that $0 < 1 < m$, and, since $m \cdot m^{-1} = 1 > 0$, it must be that $0 < m^{-1} < 1$ (see [[Mathematics/Real Analysis/Volume 1 - Elementary Calculus/Module 2 - The Space of Real Numbers/Proofs and Derivations/Chapter 1 - Basic Properties of Real Numbers/Proof of Consequences of Order Axioms\|consequences for order axioms]]). Thus, $m^{-1} \not\in  \mathbb{Z}$. The case when $m$ is a negative integer different from $-1$ reduces immediately to one already considered.
